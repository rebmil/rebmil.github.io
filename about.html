About

I'm drawn to problems that don't come with a playbook: the requirements aren't written yet, the technology is still moving, and the first job is working out what the problem actually is.

I started in science. Studying biochemistry and doing lab research at the U.S. Air Force Academy taught me patience with hard problems and honesty about what the data does and doesn't say. As an officer in the U.S. Space Force, I worked on satellite and ground systems, where I learned to stand between the engineers who know how a system works and the leaders who decide whether it gets built, and to make each side make sense to the other.

The part of that work I liked most was building. When a workflow is slowing people down, my instinct is to talk to the people living with it, sketch what better looks like, build a first version myself, and keep refining it with them. Lately that has meant AI agents: tools that take real work off people's plates and change how an organization runs day to day.

I'm now an MBA candidate at Stanford GSB, learning to turn that instinct into products: setting a roadmap, making the tradeoffs, and making the case to the people who fund it. Longer term, I want to bring AI back to where I started, in computational biology.
