Welcome, and thank you for the click!

The most important thing you should know about me is my anchor story. When I was seven, my friends and I went canoeing beyond our depth. We ended up getting stranded on a dock when our anchor broke off at the bottom of the lake. After a brief group panic, I looked down at the lake bottom, jumped off the dock, and swam up with the anchor. This childhood story made me realize my instinct, in both work and life, is to jump and swim toward the anchor.

I started in science in undergrad. Studying biochemistry and doing extensive lab research at the U.S. Air Force Academy taught me that I do not belong in a lab full time, and that decisions should ride on concrete data. As an officer in the U.S. Space Force, I worked on satellite and ground systems, where I learned to stand between the engineers who know how a system works and the leaders who decide whether it gets built, and to make each side make sense to the other.

The best part of my job was the ability to actualize constant growth and learning. Whether it was a broken workflow, an issue with a stakeholder, or a technical hurdle, I wanted to be in the problem, actively working to guide the team toward a solution.

Now, as an MBA candidate at Stanford GSB, I am learning how to apply that team mindset in large corporate and commercial environments. Longer term, I want to bring AI back to where I started, in computational biology.
